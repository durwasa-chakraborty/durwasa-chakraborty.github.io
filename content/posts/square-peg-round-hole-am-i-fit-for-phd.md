> *"Before you start some work, always ask yourself three questions ::
> Why am I doing it? What might the results be? Will I be successful?"*
> attributed to Chanakya, and quoted to me by
> {{< mention "Anup Mishra" "https://www.linkedin.com/in/anupm5999/" >}}

> This piece is mine :: the notes, the confessions, the self-diagnosis. I used
> an LLM to fix my spelling and to help me turn a wall of run-on sentences
> into paragraphs a stranger could survive.

Ideally I should have asked the question in the title a long, long time ago.
Around 2023, say, while I was still packing.

Here is the ledger, since a ledger is the honest way to open this. On one side:
a paycheque with a comma in a pleasant place, an apartment in downtown Seattle
and then in Burnaby, a porch, and a piano that arrived under the listing's
casual heading of *well furnished*. A Nissan Rogue in the parking spot. On the
other side: a room in Mahanadi hostel with no fan, and a RockRider bicycle.

I signed that deal voluntarily. Nobody made me. I told myself, and anyone at the
farewell who asked, that I was trading money for intellectual freedom, and I
meant it. Then I got into the PhD program, sat my comprehensive exam, performed
badly, sat it again, performed badly again, and the question that should have
been asked while the boxes were still open came and sat on my chest at 2 a.m.

**Am I fit for a PhD?**

---

## Industry lets you reason lazily

Amazon has a leadership principle called *Dive Deep*. I can recite it. I can
also tell you that in five years I mostly did not do it, and that the system
around me was engineered so that not doing it was the correct professional
choice.

A service is down. Is it mine? No? Then I do not need to know *why*. That is not
negligence, that is the org chart working as designed. It is abstraction,
computer science's single greatest gift, aimed at people instead of at code. You
are handed an interface, a runbook, an escalation path, and a firefighter access
credential that expires in four hours; and the whole apparatus is tuned to
convert a question into a ticket before the question can become a curiosity.

And where, genuinely, is the time? There is on-call. There is the escalation.
There is the postmortem for the escalation, and the action items from the
postmortem, and the sprint that absorbs the action items. Depth is not forbidden
in industry. It is simply never the thing on fire.

The strange part is that back then, I was confident I would make a good PhD
student. My reasoning was: I am curious about everything, and everything
non-deterministic has an explanation waiting underneath it, and I am the sort of
person who wants the explanation. That felt like the whole job description.

## A detour through Jabalpur, God, and arrogance

I am a religious person, and I have never been able to make atheism sit still in
my head as a purely scientific position.

Osho, who is from {{< mention "Jabalpur" "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur" >}}
just as I am, argues that belief and ritual are a waste of a life, and everyone
adores Osho for saying it. Richard Dawkins puts it more precisely
{{< mention "in conversation" "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U0Xn60Zw03A" >}}
:: the probability of God is on the order of the probability of leprechauns or
werewolves. Non-zero. Just very, very unlikely.

I have never been able to shake the feeling that a good part of atheism runs on
something else. Not evidence, but posture: *we don't know yet, but we will know
eventually; we always have.* That sentence is doing enormous unpaid work. It
promises a future receipt for a present certainty. It is, I think, a kind of
arrogance ; the same arrogance that let me believe curiosity alone would carry
me through a doctorate. I will understand everything. I will find the answer to
everything. I only need to want it badly enough.

Comprehensive exam, attempt II, disagreed.

## The roof of the hostel room

I want to be careful here, because this is the paragraph where a reader decides
whether they are reading self-pity.

It is not. This is the conclusion of several hours spent lying on a hostel bed
staring at the ceiling, trying to hold two things apart that look identical from
the inside: the sunk cost fallacy, and a legitimate early exit. Sunk cost tells
you to stay because of what you have already burned; the early exit tells you to
leave because the remaining road does not lead where you thought. They give
opposite advice and they wear the same face, and at 2 a.m. the only way to tell
them apart is to be brutally specific about what is actually broken.

So, specifically. I know a great many technologies. I know the *whats*. Give me
a system and I will tell you what it does, what it is called, what it competes
with, and which conference talk to watch. Where I have hit a wall is the *whys*,
and the wall is made of memory.

I forget. I forget constantly. I cannot hold last semester's material without a
lookup, let alone the computer science I read a decade ago. I cannot, sitting
here, reconstruct from first principles why Python 2's `xrange` behaves the way
it does against `range` ; I remember *that* one materialises a list and one
hands you a lazy object, and I remember which is which only because I have typed
it a few hundred times. The mechanism underneath, the actual reason, has to be
re-derived every single time, as though I had never learnt it.

My professor's response to this, when I confessed it, was the most useful
sentence anyone has said to me this year: **internalise the whys**. The whys do
not consume memory bandwidth. A *what* is a fact you have to store. A *why* is a
generator that regenerates the facts on demand. I had spent a decade filling a
cache and never once building the function.

## Half a decade of easy lookup

My hypothesis, and it is only a hypothesis, is that the lookup did this to me.

Want a query, Google it. Want a shell incantation, AskUbuntu. Want a snippet,
Stack Overflow. Want to know why a particular driver refuses to install, dig up
some fossilised IRC archive from 2009 and read a stranger's answer from a
lifetime ago. Every one of these worked. Every one of these shipped. And every
one of these quietly taught my brain that retention was somebody else's
responsibility, until the brain took the hint and became a volatile resource ::
correct while powered, empty on reboot.

I was never a brilliant student anyway. Class X went well. Some stretch of
engineering went well. Nothing in that record has ever screamed genius, and I
have made my peace with the record. But I was curious, and I am curious, and I
expect I will die curious.

Which brings the uncomfortable question :: **is curiosity satiated by a PhD?**

No. Honestly, no. Curiosity is satisfied by an afternoon and a good enough
search engine. Nobody needs six years and a committee for curiosity.

So if the stated reason for coming here is not the real reason, the decision
deserves to be re-audited from the top. Which is where Anup's three questions
come in.

---

## Why am I doing it?

I want to teach. That is the whole of it.

Not "I want a faculty position", though that is the shape it takes on paper.
I want to be the person in the room who has patience, and who knows the whats,
the whys, the hows, and the several different roads that arrive at each of them,
because a student never comes at a concept from the road you planned for.

And I want the other thing academia has that industry structurally cannot give
me :: the right to spend six months on something whose utility is laughable.

Here is the concrete daydream. I notice a drift in how a piece of code is
written. The clean fix is not a patch, it is a small language ; a DSL, a
compiler pass, a language-level expression that makes the bad state
unrepresentable. In industry I know exactly what happens next, because I have
watched it happen. A senior engineer smiles at me, kindly, and says no. Not
because the idea is wrong, but because the time investment is bizarre against
the return, and they are right to say it. The economics are sound. The
economics are also the cage.

You can only build the beautiful useless thing when that kind of money is not in
the room.

## What might the results be?

This is where my answer stops being modest, so let me just say it.

Deep expertise compounds into something the market does not price properly. You
build a body of work, then a community around the work, and eventually a
gravity: people think twice before letting you go, because letting you go costs
them the whole neighbourhood you built. Call it soft power. Call it, in my more
grandiose moments, reverse colonisation ; the quiet inversion where the centre
of a field has to come to you.

And the reason I want it is not the honorifics.

I have watched too many capable people leave a country not because they were
finished with it, but because a visa said so. When they come home, we tell a
story about how they *chose* to return. Some of them did. Many of them
sacrificed, and we papered over the sacrifice with the vocabulary of choice.
**Choice is a luxury.** Not everyone is issued one. I would like to help build
the kind of place, the kind of lab, the kind of soft power, where coming back is
genuinely a decision and not a deportation with good manners.

That is the benefit. And the only door I can see to that room is a doctorate.
So the first two questions resolve cleanly, which is more than I expected when I
started writing this.

## Am I capable of what is required?

Right now, every bone in my body says **no**.

I have some cold comfort available :: capability is usually a function of time,
and with time everything changes. But I do not have age on my side the way my
classmates do, and the honest reading of the evidence is that capability, past a
point, is largely cemented. I know the word neuroplasticity. I know it is more
optimistic than my mood. I am not going to hide behind a nice piece of biology.

Two out of three, then. The why is answered. The benefit is answered. The
capability is a resounding no in the present tense, and the entire bet of the
next few years is whether a sufficiently strong *why* can drag a mediocre
*capable* through the trials and tribulations of the thing.

That is the whole gamble, stated plainly. It is a worse gamble than the one I
thought I was making in 2023, when I believed curiosity was a qualification.

I am taking it anyway.

Hopefully, same time next year, this page has some answers on it that I actually
earned. And if it does not, at least it will have the ledger, honestly kept, and
a record of a man who could still tell the difference between a sunk cost and a
road that ran out.

## A note on the photograph

I put that picture at the top for two reasons.

The first is the duality it caught by accident. On the left-hand page it takes
most of a sheet of careful β-reduction, Church numerals, a plus function of
λm.λn.λf.λx. m f (n f x), and a chain of substitutions I had to walk three
times, to establish that **1 + 2 = 3**. A four-year-old will hand you that fact
for free. On the right-hand page, in the same ink, on the same evening, the
same hand writes in plain English that it is not sure it belongs here.

That is the shape of this life, both halves of it visible at once. The
enormous machinery required to make the obvious *provable*, sitting a
spine-width away from the ordinary difficulty of making the personal *sayable*.
I used to think one of those pages was the real work and the other was
procrastination. I no longer think that. They are the same discipline pointed
in two directions :: refusing to accept a thing just because everybody already
believes it.

The second reason is plainer. That right-hand page is this essay, in
longhand, before anything was done to it.

I am not going to pretend the version above came out of me in that shape. It
did not. The first draft never makes it to production ; that is what makes it a
first draft. I fixed the spelling, cut the parts that said the same thing
three times, and let a machine break a wall of run-on sentences into paragraphs
a stranger could survive. That is stated at the top of this post and I will
state it again here.

But the thinking happened in ink, at a desk, on a night when I did not know
whether I would publish any of it. Nothing in here was generated. It was
written, badly, and then repaired.

So if the temptation arises to file this under AI slop :: the notebook is right
there in the photograph. Go on, read it. The sentences are worse than the ones
above ; the handwriting is not. That is the one thing on this page I refuse to
be modest about. Both of them are mine.
